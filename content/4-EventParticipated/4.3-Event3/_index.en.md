**Program:** First Cloud AI Journey – FCAJ

**Role in the Event:** Attendee and viewer

## Event Objectives

The **FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week: Show Up. Build. Pitch. WIN!** event was organized to provide teams that had participated in Agentic AI Build Week with an opportunity to introduce their projects and share practical experiences with members of the First Cloud AI Journey program.

This was not the official competition session of Agentic AI Build Week. Instead, the event was organized as a project-sharing session in which the teams presented their journey of selecting a problem, building an AI product, working as a team, preparing a product demonstration, and pitching their solutions during the competition.

The main objectives of the event included:

* Introducing projects developed during Agentic AI Build Week.
* Helping FCAJ members gain a better understanding of the process of participating in a hackathon.
* Sharing experience in selecting ideas and defining an appropriate product scope.
* Demonstrating how teams developed Agentic AI products within a limited period.
* Sharing challenges encountered during system development, integration, and testing.
* Helping viewers understand how to prepare a product demonstration and pitch.
* Motivating FCAJ members to participate in future technology competitions and practical projects.
* Connecting Cloud and AI knowledge with the development of a complete product.

## Event Description

**FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week** was a networking and experience-sharing session for FCAJ members. During the event, representatives of teams that had participated in Agentic AI Build Week introduced the projects they had developed.

The speakers presented the real-world problems addressed by their projects, target user groups, proposed solutions, the role of AI agents, the technologies used, and the results achieved by their teams.

In addition to introducing their products, the teams also shared their journey from an initial idea to a working prototype. The content covered idea discussions, architecture design, task allocation, programming, system integration, testing, debugging, and presentation preparation.

Through the teams' practical experiences, FCAJ participants were able to gain a clearer understanding of a hackathon environment in which time, human resources, and technical resources are limited.

## Main Event Content

### 1. Introduction to Agentic AI Build Week

The first part of the event provided an overview of Agentic AI Build Week and the participation journey of the teams.

During the competition, participants had to form teams, select a real-world problem, propose an AI-based solution, develop a working prototype, prepare a product demonstration, and present their project to judges or mentors.

This process required not only programming knowledge but also product thinking, teamwork, time-management skills, and presentation skills.

### 2. Team Project Presentations

This was the main part of the sharing session. The teams presented:

* The real-world problem that the project aimed to solve.
* The target users of the product.
* The core idea and value of the solution.
* The role of the AI agent in the system.
* The main workflow of the product.
* The features successfully implemented by the team.
* The Cloud technologies and services used.
* The overall system architecture.
* The results achieved and possible future improvements.

Through these presentations, I realized that a successful project does not necessarily need to use a large number of technologies. The most important requirement is to identify the correct problem and demonstrate the value that the product provides.

### 3. Product Development Process

The teams shared how they transformed their initial ideas into working prototypes.

The development process generally included:

1. Identifying the problem that needed to be solved.
2. Analyzing the target users.
3. Proposing and evaluating possible ideas.
4. Defining the scope of the minimum viable product.
5. Designing the system architecture.
6. Assigning tasks to team members.
7. Programming and integrating system components.
8. Testing and fixing technical issues.
9. Preparing data for the demonstration.
10. Preparing and practicing the pitch.

This sharing session helped me understand that behind a demonstration lasting only a few minutes were many hours of discussion, programming, testing, and product refinement.

### 4. Challenges During the Development Process

Some of the challenges mentioned by the teams included:

* Very limited product-development time.
* Difficulty selecting an idea that was both creative and feasible.
* An initial project scope that was too large.
* Changes to the idea or architecture during the development process.
* Integration errors between different system components.
* Inaccurate or unstable results generated by AI agents.
* Differences in skills and experience among team members.
* Pressure to complete the product and prepare the presentation.
* The risk of technical failures during a live demonstration.

These challenges showed that participants need to be adaptable, learn quickly, and collaborate effectively with other team members.

### 5. Teamwork Experience

Through the teams' sharing, I realized that clearly assigning roles is an important factor in project development.

Each member may be responsible for one or more tasks, such as:

* Analyzing the problem and developing the project idea.
* Designing the user interface.
* Developing the backend and APIs.
* Integrating AI agents or AI models.
* Designing the database.
* Deploying the system to the Cloud.
* Testing and debugging.
* Preparing slides, the product demonstration, and the pitch.

However, team members should not work completely independently. The team needs to communicate regularly, provide progress updates, and integrate the product early to reduce errors during the final stage.

### 6. Demo Preparation Experience

A good demonstration should focus on one main user workflow that is understandable and stable.

The teams should:

* Select a demonstration scenario that clearly presents the value of the product.
* Prepare input data in advance.
* Check APIs, databases, and AI services.
* Remove unnecessary steps from the demonstration.
* Prepare a backup plan in case the system or network experiences problems.
* Clearly assign the presenter and the person responsible for controlling the demonstration.

I realized that a product with fewer but stable features can be more convincing than a product with many features that frequently fail.

### 7. Pitch Preparation Experience

The pitch should help the audience understand both the technical value and the practical value of the product.

A good pitch should answer the following questions:

* What problem does the project solve?
* Who experiences this problem?
* How does the team's solution work?
* Why is AI appropriate for this problem?
* What benefits does the product provide?
* What features has the team successfully completed?
* What makes the project different?
* How can the project be developed in the future?

The event helped me understand that a pitch should not only list frameworks, AI models, or AWS services. The most important content includes the problem, the solution, the value, and the practical results.

### 8. Discussion and Question-and-Answer Session

During the discussion session, FCAJ participants had the opportunity to ask the teams about:

* How they selected their project topics.
* How they found and formed their teams.
* How responsibilities were assigned.
* The technologies used in the projects.
* Difficulties encountered while integrating AI.
* Time management during the hackathon.
* Demo preparation.
* Presenting and answering questions from the judges.

The question-and-answer session provided practical experience that viewers could not obtain by only observing the final products.

After attending the event, I achieved the following learning outcomes:

### Better Understanding of Agentic AI

I learned that an AI agent is not only a chatbot that answers questions. An AI agent can receive a goal, analyze information, select tools, access data, and perform multiple steps to complete a task.

### Understanding the Practical Project Development Process

I gained a clearer understanding of the process from problem identification and solution design to MVP development, testing, deployment, product demonstration, and pitching.

### Developing Product Thinking

I realized that a project should begin with a user problem rather than starting only because the team wants to use a new technology.

### Understanding How to Define an MVP Scope

I learned that teams should prioritize core features that can demonstrate the value of the product within a limited period.

### Understanding the Importance of Teamwork

The event helped me better understand the importance of task allocation, communication, progress updates, and mutual support during product development.

### Improving My Understanding of Demonstrations and Presentations

I learned that a stable product demonstration and a clear pitch play an important role in helping other people understand and evaluate a project.

### Increasing My Motivation to Learn

After the event, I became more motivated to study AWS, explore Agentic AI, and actively participate in future technology competitions.

## Lessons Learned

* Start with a real-world problem instead of starting with technology.
* Select an idea that matches the team's available time, skills, and resources.
* Clearly define the MVP scope at the beginning.
* Complete the core user workflow before adding optional features.
* Assign responsibilities clearly while maintaining regular collaboration.
* Integrate system components early.
* Keep the demonstration short, understandable, and stable.
* Focus the pitch on the problem, solution, and product value.
* Prepare a backup plan for the demonstration.
* Feedback from mentors and participants is an important source of information for improving the project.
* Participants do not need to know everything before joining a hackathon.
* Experimenting, making mistakes, and debugging are important parts of the learning process.

## Personal Experience

Attending the **FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week: Show Up. Build. Pitch. WIN!** event gave me a more realistic perspective on participating in a hackathon.

Before attending the event, I believed that hackathons mainly required teams to code quickly and implement as many features as possible. However, after listening to the teams' experiences, I realized that selecting the right problem, defining a realistic scope, and organizing the work effectively are equally important.

I was especially impressed by how the teams transformed their initial ideas into products that could be demonstrated. Behind a short product demonstration were many activities, including problem analysis, architecture design, task allocation, system integration, testing, and debugging.

The event also improved my understanding of Agentic AI. I learned that an AI agent can perform a sequence of actions to achieve a goal instead of only answering an individual question. However, when developing an AI agent, developers must also consider accuracy, data security, access permissions, cost, and control over the system's actions.

Another important lesson was that a project does not need to be extremely large to provide value. A product that focuses on a specific problem, has clearly identified users, and provides a stable demonstration can be more convincing than a complex but incomplete system.

After attending the event, I became more motivated to participate in hackathons and develop practical projects. I understood that I do not need to master every technology before getting started. Actively participating, experimenting, making mistakes, and receiving feedback are important steps in developing my skills.
